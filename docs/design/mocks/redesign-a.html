Direction A — Stage

One page, no new navigation. The page column stops at nothing: content fills the window up to a comfortable measure, and every quiz becomes a two-pane stage. The instrument or the staff takes the wide left pane and scales with the window; the question, the answers and, after answering, the feedback live in a fixed right column, so nothing ever covers the question and Continue never moves.

Home turns the 25-topic scroll into a course board: the Continue band across the top, then the four modules side by side, each with its topic list, a progress ring and its Review weak spots button. On a maximized 1920 window the whole course is visible without scrolling. Lessons split the same way: step list and text on the left, a large visual panel on the right, with Previous/Next under the text instead of 700 px away at the bottom of the window.

HomeCourse board: four module columns at 1600 px and up, two columns from 1000 px, one column with collapsible modules on phones. The 1100 px frame is the Portuguese check: the 45-character title wraps to two lines and the row stays 56 px high.

Lesson stepStep list with titles replaces the five-pixel strip: the learner sees the six steps and that “On your guitar” closes each lesson. The visual panel is as wide as the window allows; Listen buttons sit under the diagram they play.

Quiz — fretboard tap questionThe target string (string 6) is highlighted with a lane and a thicker string. On desktop a keyboard focus ring shows on fret 5 of that string. The right column holds the prompt and the hint; the answer is taken on tap.

Quiz — feedback after a wrong tapNothing slides over the question. The solution is drawn on the same board (route E–F–G–A–B–C–D, the wrong tap marked ✗); the column shows the verdict, the answer, the explanation, Listen, tab and staff, and Continue pinned at the bottom with its Enter hint.

Quiz — multiple choice with a staffThe staff renders at twice today’s size (VexFlow accepts the width). Answers stack in the column with key hints 1–4; the hovered choice shows the accent border. A timed round shows the ring in the progress row.

ResultsScore and actions left, missed rounds right. A Lesson button joins Play again, so a bad score has a way back to the explanation. Misses show ✗ and ✓ icons, not only red and green.

StatsFour tiles (the fourth is “due for review”), then one card per module in a grid, with a column header row, tabular numerals and a “low” label under scores below 60 %. Reset buttons appear only on topics that have data.

TunerA proper readout: the string name in the readout face, the direction in words and an arrow, a 22 px meter with ticks at ±25 cents, and the cents value in numerals. Reference strings become a vertical list with play icons on wide screens.

SettingsStill a centered dialog on desktop and a bottom sheet on phones, with the desktop-only “This computer” group and the Progress group. Width grows to 620 px on wide screens so the segmented controls breathe.

Trade-offsWhat this direction costs, and where it is weaker than the others.

Two layouts for the quiz

The quiz has a two-pane layout from 1000 px and the stacked one below; feedback is “in column” on one and “in flow” on the other. GameShell renders both from the same state, but tests and the focus order need both paths.

Wide boards need bigger text

Scaling the 560 px SVG to 1160 px scales fret numbers to 22 px and markers to 44 px, which is fine; but the string labels (12 px → 25 px) start to look heavy. The spec caps the board at 1160 px and sets marker text to 10–11 units.

Home is dense on 1366 × 768

Two module columns put Module 1’s ten topics under the fold; the board still scrolls about one screen. Collapsing finished modules (as today) helps.

No global navigation

Stats, Tuner and Settings stay as icons; there is still no jump from a quiz to another topic. A is the least change: it accepts that the app is “pick a topic, play it”.
