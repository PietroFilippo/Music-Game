Direction B — Rail

A persistent left rail turns the course into places. Each module is a destination with a progress ring and a due-count badge, Continue lives at the top of the rail, and Stats, Tuner and Settings are one click away from anywhere. The main area shows one module at a time as a generous list with per-topic progress bars, so the long scroll disappears without hiding the course.

During lessons and quizzes the rail collapses to a 64 px strip (Back, sound, settings), the visual takes the centre of the window at up to 1200 px, and feedback docks as a right-hand drawer that pushes the content rather than covering it. Phones get module chips under the wordmark and otherwise share Direction A’s single column.

Trade-offsWhat this direction costs, and where it is weaker than the others.

Most new code

A rail, module routes, a collapsed rail state, a drawer component and a module-page layout are all new. The app has no router today; module selection becomes state in App.tsx.

Less overview

Only one module’s topics are visible at a time. The rings help, but seeing the 25 topics requires four clicks.

The drawer squeezes the board

At 1100 px the neck drops to about 540 px when feedback opens, smaller than today, exactly when the learner compares their tap with the answer. A keeps the board size constant.

Two chrome systems

The rail is a second place for Settings, Sound and Back; the top bar still exists for titles. Drag regions for the desktop window need the rail header too.
