Direction C — Desk

Paper above, instrument below. The window is split like a practice desk: the upper area is the “paper” (prompt, staff, tab, answers, explanations) and a full-width instrument strip at the bottom holds the neck, pinned, as wide as the window. Reading happens above; finding happens below. Lessons work the same way, and the Tuner turns the strip into six string lanes you can tap to hear and that light up when heard.

Home becomes a course sheet: all 25 topics in a table (two tables side by side at 1600 px and up) with best and last scores, plus a Today panel with Continue, due reviews and recent plays. Stats is the same table with more columns. Settings is a full page in two columns on desktop.

Trade-offsWhat this direction costs, and where it is weaker than the others.

Huge boards need their own typography

At 1500 px the SVG scales labels to about 32 px and markers to 60 px. The board would need a size-aware mode (smaller unit sizes for wide widths), which Fretboard.tsx does not have.

Half the topics have no neck

Module 1 and many Module 3–4 questions show a staff, a keyboard or text. The strip is empty or absent for them, so the layout metaphor only holds for part of the course.

Vertical squeeze on the default window

At 1100 × 720 the strip takes a third of the height; the paper above has room for a prompt and a short explanation only. Long explanations scroll.

Phones are a different app

A pinned strip does not fit a phone, so phones use Direction A. Two mental models for one product.
