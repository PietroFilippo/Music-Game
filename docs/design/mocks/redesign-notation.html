Notation — round 2 of the redesign

musicgame · UI redesign · indexA — StageBCnotation

Direction A is approved. The owner’s one change: the white notation cards (tab and staff in quiz feedback, every staff diagram in the lessons) glare on the dark UI and look pasted in; notation should belong to the same visual language as the app’s own fretboard drawing, and no lesson may get harder to read.

Every drawing on this board is real VexFlow 4 output (Bravura font, SVG backend) from a throwaway prototype served by the Vite dev server: notation-proto/index.html, with prototype copies of the five notation components in notation-proto/notation.tsx. The fretboards are the real Fretboard.tsx. Screens are the approved A layout at 1920 × 1032 (stored at 50 %), 1100 × 720 and 390 × 844; the detail crops are 1 : 1 or 2 : 1. Nothing under src/ changed. Write-up: docs/design/REDESIGN.md, section “Notation”.

Three directions, one component stripThe same notation in today’s card, and in the three proposals, rendered side by side at 1 : 1 on the page background (first row) and inside a lesson panel. Column 1 is today. Columns 2–4 are the proposals.

N3 · Quiet paper (conservative fallback)

Keeps the card, loses the glare: a warm grey paper (#dcd9cf, 12.7 : 1 against the page instead of 16.7 : 1 today), no border, 12 px radius, ink #1b1e1b. Highlight gets the halo ring too. Shown because it is the smallest change (two tokens and a CSS rule), not because it is recommended: the card is still a pasted-in rectangle, the highlight green is a different green from the neck’s mint, and the eye still re-adapts between the card and the neck on the screens that show both.

N1 · Ink on slate (recommended as the base)

No card. The stave and tab lines are drawn in a grey one step below the neck’s strings (#7c867e), the glyphs in the foreground colour, the highlighted note and its ledger lines in the same mint the fretboard markers use, with a halo ring and a bold label, and the line and space numbers in the muted label colour. Notation sits directly on the panel or the page, like the neck does. Comprehension gain: one colour means “this note” everywhere (the staff note, its label, the neck marker, the lit string); nothing competes with the glyphs; and lessons that show a staff above a neck no longer flip between a bright card and a dark board.

N2 · Ink + linked tab (recommended wherever a neck or a position is shown)

N1, plus the tab drawn in the neck’s own geometry: six lines in the string grey, thicker towards string 6 exactly like the board (1 + 0.3 × string), the played string lit in mint with the fret number on it, and the string names E B G D A E at the left in the same order as the board’s labels. The TAB sign stays, so the learner still recognises real tablature. Comprehension gain: the tab is visibly a picture of the six strings the learner just tapped, in the same orientation (string 1 on top), which is the single most common tab confusion; the lit line answers “which string?” before the number answers “which fret?”; in the rhythm measure the lit string shows the whole phrase is on one string.

Recommendation: build N1 as the base for every notation component, and N2’s linked tab for GuitarTab, PositionNotation and the tab half of MeasureNotation. N3 only if the owner wants to keep a paper look as a brand trait.

Minimum sizes at 390 px (measured in the prototype)

  • Line spacing 10 px (VexFlow default; unchanged from today).
  • Whole-note head about 11 × 7 px; ledger lines 1 px, glyph colour (14.7 : 1).
  • Tab number 11 pt ≈ 15 px, readout face, bold.
  • String names 11 px bold, muted (6.3 : 1); the lit one in mint.
  • Line and space numbers 12 px, readout face.
  • Note labels 13 px (bold when highlighted).
  • Captions “Tab” / “Staff (written)” 13 px, sentence case (round 1, R3).

Highlight without colour alone

  • A 1.5 px ring at radius 10.5 around the notehead with a 18 % mint fill: a shape, visible in greyscale.
  • The label under the note goes bold (500 → 700).
  • In the tab the lit string is 3 px against 1–2.5 px, and the number sits on it.
  • The halo is drawn before the note, so the notehead stays crisp over it.

What stays the same for the learner

  • Same VexFlow layout, clefs, note positions, ledger lines, numbering and labels as today — only the colours and the surface change; the tab gains string names.
  • Guitar facts kept: tab line 1 (top) is string 1; written pitch = sounding + 12 (D3 → D4, E2 → E3, E3 → E4).
  • The piano keyboard keeps white keys (it is a picture of a real object) but drops to the quiet off-white (#e3e1d8) with the page colour for black keys. Secondary; see the strip.

Decisions for the owner

1. Direction

N1 + N2 (recommended), N1 alone (plain tab everywhere), or N3 quiet paper.

2. Linked tab scope

Everywhere a tab is drawn (recommended: feedback, explorer, Strings and Tuning, Staff/Tab/Rhythm lesson and quiz), or only where a neck is on the same screen.

3. Highlight the asked note in the rhythm quiz

Halo on the staff and lit string in the tab for “tab note n” questions (recommended), or keep the measure neutral as today.

4. Pair size

260 / 200 / 156 px in the feedback column (recommended, possible without card chrome) or the A spec’s 220 / 180 / 156.

5. Keyboard

Quiet off-white keys (recommended) or keep #fafafa.
